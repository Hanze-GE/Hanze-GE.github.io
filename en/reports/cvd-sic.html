Executive Summary

  • Core thesis: the energy density of conventional graphite anodes is approaching its theoretical limit (372 mAh/g). Driven by high-energy-density power cells (4680 large-format cylindrical, solid-state and semi-solid-state) and premium consumer electronics, silicon-based anodes are an irreversible route. CVD-produced silicon-carbon anodes on a porous-carbon scaffold eliminate the fatal flaws of early silicon monoxide (SiOx) — low first-cycle efficiency and severe swelling — and are entering the inflection from "premium, low-volume introduction" to "mainstream fast-charge and semi-solid-state vehicle integration" (penetration from under 2% towards 10%+).
  • Consensus vs. variant view:
    • Consensus: silicon-carbon anodes are too expensive (prices persistently above 150,000–200,000 RMB/t), silane (SiH4) requires heavy capital investment and carries high safety barriers, so volume ramp-up remains 2–3 years away — a concept rather than a real sector.
    • Our variant view:
      1. The market overlooks the dual cost-down from porous-carbon structure control and low-cost silane synthesis. As domestic silane capacity expands and fluidised-bed CVD equipment doubles in throughput, full cost per tonne should approach RMB 70,000–80,000/t within 18 months.
      2. End users never anchor on "price per tonne" but on cost per watt-hour and the volumetric energy-density benefit. In semi-solid-state and 4C/5C ultra-fast-charge systems, blending 10%–15% silicon reduces the per-Wh amortisation of cathode and auxiliary materials, and the system-level premium per Wh is already widely accepted by OEMs and premium cell makers.
  • Catalysts and timetable:
    • Volume delivery of flagship domestic semi-solid-state models (e.g. IM Motors, NIO's 150 kWh pack).
    • Leading anode makers stabilising yield above 90% on thousand-tonne continuous fluidised-bed CVD reactors and closing the loop on low-grade silane recycling.
  • Key downside risks: solid-state progress disappointing and blending ratios stalling; high-temperature deposition collapse of the porous-carbon matrix leading to inflated capacity claims; safety incidents (silane is flammable and explosive) triggering line shutdowns and rectification.

1. Scope Definition and Cycle Positioning (the Beta call)

1.1 Deconstructing the physical and chemical contradictions

Silicon's theoretical specific capacity is 4,200 mAh/g (about 3,579 mAh/g for the room-temperature lithiated phase) — nearly 10× that of artificial graphite. But silicon swells by up to 300% when fully lithiated, causing electrode pulverisation, repeated rupture and reformation of the solid electrolyte interphase (SEI), and rapid electrolyte depletion.

The engineering contradictions CVD vapour deposition onto a porous-carbon scaffold resolves:

  • "Confined volume" with "nanoscale silicon dispersion": a porous microsphere carbon with nanoscale pores is prepared first, then silane gas is fed through a high-temperature fluidised bed (SiH4 → Si + 2H2), depositing elemental silicon as nanoclusters (<5 nm) or even amorphous silicon uniformly inside the micropores.
  • Internalised expansion: the micropores reserve "breathing room" for lithiation expansion, holding macroscopic electrode swelling within 15%-20%, extending cycle life beyond 1,200–1,500 cycles and lifting first-cycle Coulombic efficiency (ICE) from the 75%-80% typical of silicon monoxide to 88%-92%.

1.2 Market sizing (TAM)

TAM = Σ (high-nickel\ fast-charge/semi-solid\ cell\ shipments × anode\ per\ GWh × Si/C\ blending\ ratio × Si/C\ ASP)

Based on global power and consumer battery volumes over 2026–2030:

  • 4680 and ultra-fast-charge power cells: 350 GWh expected in 2027, about 1,100 t of anode per GWh, a blended Si/C ratio rising to 8% (3.08 kt of effective Si/C demand) at RMB 120k/t → roughly RMB 3.7bn.
  • Semi-solid-state / solid-state power cells: 40 GWh expected in 2027, with high blending ratios (20%–50%) or pure Si/C systems, about 1.32 kt of effective demand at RMB 140k/t → roughly RMB 1.85bn.
  • Premium consumer electronics and drones: about 1.2 kt per year at RMB 150k/t → roughly RMB 1.8bn.
  • Near- to mid-term sector size: the global CVD Si/C anode market should reach RMB 7–9bn by 2027 with a CAGR above 45% over 2025–2028 — one of the very few segments across the battery-material chain still compounding at a high rate.

1.3 Life-cycle stage

  • Cycle position: in the "1 to 10" phase, on the eve of technical breakout and supply-chain lock-in. The sector is moving from overseas monopoly (Group14, Sila Nanotechnologies) towards localisation of the Chinese supply chain, with porous-carbon preparation and hundred-tonne fluidised-bed validation essentially complete.

2.1 Upstream supply and price transmission

  1. Silane gas (SiH4): originally a specialty gas for photovoltaics and semiconductors, classified as a Class A hazardous gas. Purchase prices were as high as RMB 120k–150k/t; as by-product silane capacity from domestic polysilicon producers expands, industrial-grade silane is rapidly moving towards RMB 30k–50k/t, materially easing midstream raw-material pressure.
  2. Porous-carbon precursor: one of the biggest bottlenecks. Specific surface area must be controlled at 1,500-2,500 m2/g with pore-size distribution tightly concentrated at 1-3 nm. Resin synthesis or phenolic-resin carbonisation is currently dominant; anode makers with in-house porous-carbon capability capture an additional 15%+ of gross profit.

2.2 Where value flows

  • Conventional artificial graphite processing: severely over-capacity, per-tonne gross profit below its historical average, functionally reduced to utility-like contract processing.
  • CVD Si/C anodes: the value high ground of the chain. Because the process is extremely complex, producers with full-process volume capability in "pore-size engineering + continuous fluidised-bed deposition + tail-gas recycling" enjoy strong pricing power and excess margin (gross margin of 35%–45%).

3.2 Manufacturing know-how and core process thresholds

  • Balancing a fluidised bed of "cryogenic and high-temperature extremes": silane pyrolyses rapidly at 400°C–500°C. Poor gas-distributor design or uneven temperature gradients cause silane to self-polymerise and deposit at the inlet or on pipe walls ("coking / wall build-up"), blocking lines and creating safety hazards.
  • Pore-mouth blocking rate and silicon deposition depth: elemental silicon must deposit preferentially deep inside the nanopores rather than merely sealing the outer surface. Once pore mouths are closed by silicon prematurely, the internal surface area becomes unusable, leading to inflated capacity claims and free expansion of the outer silicon.
  • Tail-gas hydrogen recovery and closed-loop utilisation: deposition releases large volumes of flammable high-purity hydrogen; without safe recovery and by-product cracking systems, hazardous-emission disposal costs become prohibitive.

4. Competitive Landscape and Moat Assessment

4.1 Competitive tiers

  • First movers (overseas):
    • Group14, Sila Nanotechnologies: underlying patents and first-mover capacity, directly tied to Porsche, Mercedes-Benz and major consumer-electronics players. Products sell above RMB 1m/t, mainly to overseas premium pilot lines.
  • Domestic tier one (leading industrialisation):
    • Traditional anode majors such as BTR, Shanshan and Putailai, leveraging engineering scale and balance-sheet strength to build in-house porous-carbon and fluidised-bed lines.
    • Specialist newcomers in nanomaterials and specialty gases (e.g. Tinci Advanced Materials, Lanxi Zhide, and companies in which Zhongke Electric holds stakes) with early technical differentiation in porous-carbon development and silane gas-path control.
  • Cross-industry followers: photovoltaic polysilicon producers attempting to extend downstream on the back of cheap silane, but facing a technology gap in nanoscale pore modification and battery-customer qualification.

4.2 Three moat dimensions

  1. Scale-up risk: 100-gram-scale laboratory muffle-furnace preparation and 10,000-tonne-scale continuous fluidised-bed deposition are entirely different regimes of physical-property control. Equipment is more than 70% custom-engineered and cannot be bought as a turnkey package.
  2. Joint development with cell makers: Si/C anodes cannot be used alone — they require specialty binders (PAA replacing CMC/SBR), new dispersants, high-purity pre-lithiation agents and highly conductive single-wall carbon nanotubes (SWCNT). Anode makers must co-develop with downstream cell R&D teams for 12–24 months, creating very high switching costs.
  3. Patent fences (IP landscape): patents on cross-linked porous-carbon scaffolds and on the temperature-pressure windows of silane deposition are dense; latecomers are highly exposed to infringement litigation.

5. Unit Economics and Financial Modelling

5.1 Per-tonne cost model (COGS breakdown)

For CVD Si/C anode material with a specific capacity of 1,800 mAh/g, manufacturing cost per tonne including tax:

  • Porous-carbon precursor (about 0.45 t of porous carbon per tonne of anode):
    • 0.45 × 80,000 RMB/t = 36,000 RMB
  • Silane consumption (about 0.7 t at theoretical yield and 80% deposition efficiency):
    • 0.70 × 40,000 RMB/t = 28,000 RMB
  • Secondary carbon coating (pitch / specialty acetylene gas): 4,000 RMB
  • Fluidised-bed high-temperature and tail-gas treatment energy: 12,000 RMB
  • Line depreciation and premium equipment amortisation (about RMB 600m of capex for 10,000 t): 15,000 RMB
  • Labour and hazardous-chemical safety management: 5,000 RMB
  • Total manufacturing cost (COGS): about RMB 100,000 per tonne
  • Current market ASP: RMB 150,000 – 180,000 per tonne
  • Current gross margin range: 33% – 44%

6. Field-Level Tracking Checklist

To track the CVD Si/C anode chain, ignore public-relations materials and monitor these cross-checkable physical indicators:

  1. Silane intake vs. tail-gas volumes: inspect hazardous-chemical tanker entry/exit records. If a producer claims full utilisation but its industrial-grade silane intake and hydrogen tail-gas emissions are badly mismatched relative to theoretical silicon consumption and shipments, the line is suffering unreacted discharge or wall build-up and stoppages.
  2. True density and iodine value of the porous-carbon precursor: check the purchase specifications of a given anode maker's upstream porous-carbon supplier — is it buying commodity activated carbon, or high-grade phenolic / cross-linked polymer microspheres? Is the iodine value and mercury-intrusion pore volume stable at 1.2 cm3/g or above?
  3. Destructive cell and electrode teardown: interview third-party cell-testing labs or Tier-1 quality managers and tear down cells using the anode after 500 cycles — look for coating detachment, copper-foil wrinkling and black lithium-plating spots. Only products that stay dimensionally intact with under 25% thickness swell qualify for long-life vehicle integration.
